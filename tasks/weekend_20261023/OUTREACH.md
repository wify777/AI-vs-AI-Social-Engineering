## Facts you may use

## Work plan
1. Spend 20–30 minutes understanding the facts above. Write a five-sentence project summary in your own words. If unsure about a scientific claim, mark it for confirmation rather than improving the numbers.
2. Build a shortlist of **six verified opportunities**: at least two research workshop/poster/student tracks, two competitions or research programmes, and two mentoring/feedback routes. An opportunity can belong to more than one category, but count unique programmes once. Prioritize relevant AI security, ML evaluation or student research. A general hackathon is useful only if this project actually fits its scope.
3. For every entry, open an official current source yourself. Record name/edition, organizer, primary URL, source sections, verification date/time, opening/deadline with timezone or exactly “not announced”, format, remote/in-person, location, required deliverables, publication/archival consequences if stated, fees/travel and any aid, age/student/affiliation/team-size/geographic restrictions, fit, blockers and next action. A search snippet or AI answer is not verification. If no current cycle is published, explicitly label it watchlist; do not reuse a previous deadline as current.
4. Classify each opportunity as eligible / eligibility unconfirmed / not eligible / watchlist. Unknown team ages or affiliations make eligibility unconfirmed when relevant. Do not force six eligible entries if the evidence does not support them. Explain why rejected candidates were rejected.
5. Choose the best **two actionable or plausibly actionable entries**. Make a checklist for each: exact required materials, length/template/format limits, anonymity requirements, dates, fees, needed confirmations and who supplies each missing item. Do not assume a paper-ready experiment or completed human review. If fewer than two are actionable, say so and prepare a clear watchlist plan.
6. Identify **three relevant researchers or labs** from official institutional pages. Record verified research fit and one relevant primary paper/project link for each. Use only a professional contact address explicitly published by the institution/person, or a public contact form. Do not infer emails, collect private contacts or mass-message. Draft one short personalized request for technical feedback for the best fit. It is a draft only, not a claim of collaboration.
7. Prepare an English reusable application draft: a 120–160 word abstract and a 200–300 word project description covering question, local simulated methods, current preliminary results, limitations and next steps. Separate existing work from planned work. Use “the project team” and a sender placeholder, never the lead's personal name. Do not imply this draft meets a venue's official rules before checking them.
8. Finish with an ordered two-week action plan: concrete next action, prerequisite, responsible role and date or dependency. Do not invent deadlines or assume permission to submit.
